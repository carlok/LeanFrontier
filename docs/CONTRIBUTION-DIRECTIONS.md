# LeanFrontier contribution directions

This document is a **non-normative planning aid** for extending the LeanFrontier corpus.

It is not part of the trusted submission contract and it does not authorize a submission by
itself. Before acting on any direction below, re-read the current
[`CONTRACT.md`](../CONTRACT.md) and submitter guidance, fetch current `main`, inspect the
accepted source modules and generated catalogue, search Mathlib and LeanFrontier for duplicate
or equivalent formulations, and revalidate any mathematical literature claim.

The purpose of this document is narrower: preserve useful, adversarially reviewed directions
for making the corpus accumulate into connected, reusable bodies of mathematics rather than a
bag of unrelated theorems.

**Completed directions are intentionally removed from this file.** The catalogue, submission
records and receiver observations are the historical record of what landed. Keeping completed
targets here as a sequence of `Status: landed` sections makes the roadmap progressively less
useful and encourages agents to rediscover already-finished work.

Likewise, this file should describe mathematical opportunities and representation boundaries,
not the state or sequencing of particular contributors' branches or pull requests.

## 1. Selection principle

Prefer a target when it does all or most of the following:

- substantively uses accepted LeanFrontier definitions or theorems rather than merely importing
  their modules;
- expresses a recognized mathematical connection, or creates reusable infrastructure with a
  clear independent purpose;
- has plausible downstream consumers after it is merged;
- strengthens the internal mathematical dependency graph for mathematical reasons rather than
  graph aesthetics;
- is not already present in Mathlib or LeanFrontier under an equivalent formulation;
- survives an adversarial boundary review before a candidate is frozen.

A useful test is:

> If this result existed, what mathematically natural theorem would become easier or newly
> possible next?

If there is no good answer, do not formalize the result merely to create an import edge.

A second test is increasingly important because several corpus clusters are now mature:

> Is this theorem adding a new interface, or is it only another short corollary of an interface
> LeanFrontier already has?

The latter should usually be left to downstream users. A recognizable theorem can still be too
small to deserve a new corpus module when the accepted API plus one Mathlib theorem already
proves it immediately.

A third test applies near open problems:

> Is the proposed module a named mathematical result or a representation with an independently
> useful contract, or is it speculative scaffolding whose only justification is that it might
> help with the conjecture later?

The corpus now contains enough open-problem-adjacent infrastructure that this distinction
matters. Prefer literature-backed intermediate theorems over indefinite machinery accumulation.

Greenfield mathematics remains welcome. Reusing the corpus is evidence of accumulation, not a
requirement to force every theorem into an existing cluster. A standalone result is a good target
when it is mathematically substantial, absent from Mathlib, represented cleanly, and likely to
be reusable later.

## 2. Where the accepted corpus now has leverage

This section describes the accepted corpus at the audited revision recorded in Section 8. It
deliberately ignores unmerged pull requests.

### Arithmetic topology: Furstenberg topology and finite quotients

The original Furstenberg topology development is now structural rather than merely
set-theoretic. The corpus has:

- the named topology `Int.furstenbergTopology` and its arithmetic-progression basis;
- clopen congruence classes, separation and absence of isolated points;
- continuity of addition and negation;
- the named `furstenbergAddGroupTopology`;
- explicit reduction maps `ℤ → ZMod n`;
- identification of arithmetic progressions with reduction fibers;
- the theorem
  `furstenbergTopology_eq_finiteQuotientTopology`, characterizing the topology as the one
  induced jointly by all nonzero finite cyclic quotients.

The next interesting boundary is no longer another separation axiom. It is the canonical
relationship with the **profinite completion of the additive group of integers**. Mathlib now
contains a generic profinite-completion construction for groups, including the additive version
generated by `to_additive), so the corpus is well placed for a genuine interface theorem rather
than a parallel ad hoc completion.

### Rational trees, Euclidean algorithms and continued fractions

The rational-tree cluster is also mature:

- Stern's diatomic sequence and its exact enumeration of positive coprime pairs;
- Calkin-Wilf binary paths and positive integer path codes;
- Stern-Brocot paths, interval bounds and mediant invariants;
- the Calkin-Wilf / Stern-Brocot path-reversal bridge;
- Farey-neighbour and Ford-circle interfaces.

What is still missing is the classical dictionary between these binary paths and the
**Euclidean algorithm / regular continued fractions**. Mathlib already has substantial continued
fraction infrastructure, including computation, continuants, convergents and rational
termination. LeanFrontier has the tree side. Connecting them would turn two mature APIs into one
reusable arithmetic interface.

### Interval dynamics

The accepted dynamics cluster now contains the full structural conjugacy, not merely a
trigonometric semiconjugacy:

- the full tent self-map of ([0,1]);
- the logistic self-map (x ↦ 4x(1-x));
- the Ulam-von Neumann map (x ↦ sin^2(πx/2));
- `ulamHomeomorph : Icc 0 1 ≃ₜ Icc 0 1`;
- the exact conjugacy between the subtype tent and logistic maps.

The next useful results should therefore be **global dynamical properties** proved once for the
simpler tent map and transported structurally. Topological transitivity is the clearest target;
topological mixing or exactness may be a better theorem if the proof and Mathlib API support it
without inventing a private dynamics vocabulary.

### Cyclotomic eight and explicit quadratic subfields

The discriminant-tower project reached its intended witness. The accepted corpus now contains:

- an explicit eighth cyclotomic field;
- elements squaring to (2) and (-2);
- their exact quadratic minimal polynomials;
- two quadratic intermediate fields corresponding to (mathbb Q(sqrt2)) and
  (mathbb Q(sqrt{-2}));
- degree, compositum, linear-disjointness and discriminant calculations;
- the resolved `CoprimalityIsLoadBearing` proposition.

That file should no longer be treated as an unfinished counterexample. Its reusable content is
an explicit small cyclotomic extension with enough concrete generators to support a clean
description of its **Galois group and complete quadratic-subfield lattice**.

### Descartes, Ford circles and inversive geometry

Three useful representations exist, but their common geometric interface is still missing:

- algebraic Descartes curvature quadruples and the Vieta reflection on one curvature;
- actual Ford circles with tangency criteria and a Farey/Ford/Descartes configuration theorem;
- generalized-circle Hermitian forms and anti-Möbius reflection in the inversive-geometry module.

The important missing object is not another theorem saying two expressions called
`reflect` are equal. It is a representation of **oriented circles / curvature-center
coordinates** rich enough to state tangency and Descartes completion geometrically.

### Markov / Farey / Stern-Brocot

This cluster has changed the most. The corpus now contains, among other things:

- the Markov equation, Vieta moves, local descent and global reachability;
- bundled states, finite walks and exact walk reversal;
- an oriented binary Markov tree;
- the Stern-Brocot path bridge, branch coverage and cyclic symmetry;
- injectivity of the full Markov state along the canonical tree;
- Fibonacci-spine formulas;
- the formal statement of the Frobenius/Markov uniqueness conjecture and its reduction to the
  canonical Stern-Brocot branch;
- monotone Markov-number labels, common-ancestor and first-divergence interfaces;
- re-rooted subtrees and exact child-label algebra;
- pairwise coprimality, sum-of-squares divisibility, square roots of (-1) modulo Markov
  numbers, mod-four information and collision factorization machinery.

This is now a **mature research substrate adjacent to an open conjecture**, not a default source
of roadmap-sized infrastructure. Further work should be justified by a named classical theorem,
a known restricted uniqueness case, or a literature-backed reduction with a clearly bounded
claim. The roadmap should not reward adding one more lemma merely because it may someday help
with uniqueness.

As an example of the new threshold, the statement that every accepted oriented Markov number is
a sum of two squares is mathematically recognizable, but the accepted modular-root theorem plus
Mathlib's `Nat.eq_sq_add_sq_of_isSquare_mod_neg_one` already makes it essentially a direct
consumer corollary. That is useful downstream mathematics, but not by itself a high-priority new
interface.

### Mature clusters that should normally be consumed, not mined

Several other areas now have enough infrastructure that easy follow-ups should not be default
targets:

- Horadam/Fibonacci/Lucas: scalar recurrences, addition laws, companion matrices, explicit
  powers, trace, determinant and characteristic polynomials;
- probability: finite, PMF and measure-theoretic Paley-Zygmund; Cantelli; Chung-Erdos;
  Kochen-Stone; pairwise Borel-Cantelli;
- change ringing: complete permutation extent plus cyclic adjacent-transposition closure;
- finite variance: the variance identity and Laguerre-Samuelson consequence;
- finite-group characters: the corpus has a concrete orthogonality layer while Mathlib has a
  substantially richer general Fourier/character framework;
- Ford/Farey local arithmetic and geometry: enough low-level determinant and tangency lemmas
  already exist that future additions should express a genuinely new synthesis.

## 3. Highest-priority directions

### A. Identify the Furstenberg topology with the profinite-completion topology on `ℤ`

**Status:** the finite-quotient topology is accepted; Mathlib has generic profinite completion.

**Primary parents:**

- `LeanFrontier.Topology.Furstenberg`;
- `LeanFrontier.Topology.FurstenbergAlgebra`;
- `LeanFrontier.Topology.FurstenbergQuotients`;
- Mathlib's `ProfiniteAddGrp.ProfiniteCompletion` API.

**Mathematical target:**

Relate the named Furstenberg topology to the canonical map from the additive group (mathbb Z)
into its profinite completion.

A strong staged development would aim for:

1. express finite-index additive subgroups of (mathbb Z) in the congruence language already
   used by `zmodReduction`;
2. compare the generic finite-quotient diagram with the cyclic quotients `ZMod n`;
3. prove that the topology induced on (mathbb Z) by the canonical profinite-completion map is
   exactly `furstenbergTopology`;
4. package the canonical map as a topological embedding with dense range under the named local
   topology;
5. only then, if Mathlib's completion/uniformity interfaces line up cleanly, identify the
   completion of the Furstenberg topological group with the profinite completion.

The topology theorem is already worthwhile even if the final completion equivalence requires
more infrastructure.

**Why this is valuable:**

It gives a conceptual endpoint to the entire Furstenberg cluster: the elementary
arithmetic-progression topology becomes the familiar profinite topology on (mathbb Z). It also
connects a hand-built elementary development to Mathlib's new categorical profinite machinery.

**Adversarial checks required:**

- keep `furstenbergTopology` named/local; do not replace the ordinary global topology on
  (mathbb Z);
- distinguish a topology induced by the canonical map from a uniform completion statement;
- prove the required cofinality/classification of finite-index subgroups rather than assuming
  all finite quotients are literally `ZMod n`;
- reuse the additive profinite-completion API generated by Mathlib instead of duplicating the
  multiplicative theory.

**Priority:** A+.

### B. Build the Stern-Brocot / continued-fraction dictionary

**Status:** both sides now have substantial accepted infrastructure, but no bridge was found in
LeanFrontier or Mathlib.

**Primary parents:**

- `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.SternBrocot` and its interval/path modules;
- `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.CalkinWilf`;
- `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.CalkinWilfSternBrocot`;
- `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.SternDiatomic`;
- Mathlib's regular continued fraction and continuant/convergent APIs.

**Mathematical target:**

For a positive reduced rational, identify the run lengths in its Stern-Brocot left/right path
with the coefficients of its finite regular continued fraction, equivalently with the quotient
sequence of the Euclidean algorithm.

A useful staged interface would include:

1. a canonical run-length encoding for nonempty Boolean paths;
2. a subtraction/division recursion showing how one run corresponds to one Euclidean quotient;
3. a normalized finite continued-fraction convention and a theorem identifying its coefficients
   with path run lengths;
4. recovery of Stern-Brocot ancestors or bounding intervals from continued-fraction
   convergents;
5. optionally, a (2×2) matrix/continuant formulation if it simplifies both sides rather than
   creating a third representation for its own sake.

**Why this is valuable:**

This is a classical connection between rational trees, Euclid's algorithm, continued fractions
and unimodular matrices. It gives the already-rich rational-tree cluster an interface to a major
Mathlib number-theory subsystem and creates obvious downstream consumers in Diophantine
approximation.

**Adversarial checks required:**

- finite continued fractions have the terminal ambiguity
  ([a_0,ldots,a_k] = [a_0,ldots,a_k-1,1]); choose and prove a canonical convention;
- align the root-to-leaf versus reversed Calkin-Wilf/Stern-Brocot path conventions before
  stating coefficient formulas;
- do not reprove Mathlib's convergent recurrence under new names;
- distinguish positive rationals below and above one cleanly, rather than hiding the first
  quotient in case splits.

**Priority:** A+.

### C. Prove global topological dynamics for the full tent map and transport it to the logistic map

**Status:** topological conjugacy is accepted; the accepted corpus does not yet package
topological transitivity or mixing for these maps.

**Primary parent:** `LeanFrontier.Dynamics.LogisticConjugacy`.

**Mathlib support:** `Mathlib.Dynamics.Transitive` contains the general topological-transitivity
framework.

**Mathematical target:**

Prove a global orbit-spreading property for the full tent map on ([0,1]), preferably at the
strongest natural level that fits existing APIs cleanly:

1. topological transitivity as the minimum target;
2. topological mixing if the interval proof can be expressed without building a large private
   framework;
3. transport the property through `ulamHomeomorph` to the parameter-four logistic map.

A direct tent-map proof should exploit dyadic subdivision / piecewise linear expansion rather
than trigonometric calculations on the logistic side.

**Downstream consumers:**

- a clean Devaney-chaos package once topological transitivity and a separately accepted
  dense-periodic-points theorem are both available;
- dense-orbit or sensitivity consequences when they are supported by an appropriate Mathlib
  theorem rather than restated ad hoc;
- reusable examples for Mathlib's abstract topological-dynamics API.

**Boundary:**

Do not submit a theorem merely named “the logistic map is chaotic”. State and prove the chosen
definition's components. If stronger properties such as exactness or mixing are used to obtain
transitivity, expose the stronger theorem when it is mathematically natural.

**Priority:** A.

### D. Describe the Galois and quadratic-subfield structure of the eighth cyclotomic field

**Status:** explicit quadratic generators and the discriminant witness are accepted.

**Primary parent:** `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.DiscriminantTowerWitness`.

**Mathematical target:**

Turn the explicit witness into a small but complete structural description of
(mathbb Q(zeta_8)):

1. construct the third classical quadratic subfield (mathbb Q(i)), alongside the accepted
   (mathbb Q(sqrt2)) and (mathbb Q(sqrt{-2}));
2. identify the relevant intersections and pairwise composita;
3. identify the Galois group with the Klein four group, preferably by reusing Mathlib's generic
   cyclotomic Galois machinery rather than reproving it from generator arithmetic;
4. prove that these are exactly the three quadratic intermediate fields.

A good final theorem should read as a statement about the subfield lattice/Galois
correspondence, not just three unrelated generator calculations.

**Why this is valuable:**

The accepted discriminant file currently uses the eighth cyclotomic field as a concrete witness.
This continuation would expose the mathematical structure that makes that witness work and
provide a reusable small example for number-field/Galois APIs.

**Adversarial checks required:**

- search Mathlib's cyclotomic Galois-group theorems at the pinned release before introducing a
  competing group model;
- separate “this explicit field is quadratic” from the global classification of quadratic
  intermediate fields;
- avoid another long chain of private opaque arithmetic lemmas when generic Galois
  correspondence can carry the classification.

**Priority:** A-/B+; medium-to-high implementation risk.

---

### E. Add a genuine curvature-center / Apollonian bridge

**Status:** algebraic Descartes, Ford-circle geometry and generalized-circle inversive geometry
are accepted, but they still use different representations.

**Primary parents:**

- `LeanFrontier.NumberTheory.DescartesCircle`;
- `LeanFrontier.Geometry.FordCircleDescartes`;
- `LeanFrontier.Geometry.InversiveGeometry`.

**Mathematical target:**

Introduce the representation needed to connect actual tangent circles with Descartes
completion. A staged development could be:

1. bundle a four-curvature vector and the Descartes quadratic form;
2. package the four coordinate Vieta reflections uniformly, preferably as linear/matrix
   transformations preserving that quadratic form;
3. introduce oriented circles or curvature-center coordinates with a clear tangency predicate;
4. prove that a geometric Descartes configuration satisfies the algebraic curvature relation;
5. prove that replacing one circle by the alternate tangent completion changes its curvature by
   the accepted Descartes reflection;
6. only after the geometric objects line up, relate that operation to an appropriate inversive
   transformation or Apollonian-group action.

**Why this is valuable:**

It would turn three nearby but currently separate modules into one actual piece of Apollonian
geometry. The matrix layer also has an independent algebraic purpose: it packages the generators
of the Apollonian group and their invariant quadratic form.

**Critical boundary:**

The existing `DescartesCircle.reflect` and `InversiveGeometry.reflect` are not interchangeable
because they share a name. One acts on a curvature coordinate; the other is an anti-Möbius map on
points. Any theorem connecting them must pass through an explicit circle/configuration
representation and prove that it describes the same geometric replacement.

**Priority:** B+ for value; high representation risk.

## 4. Conditional / research-only directions

### Markov uniqueness program

The corpus now has enough standard groundwork that “continue building machinery” is not a
sufficient target description.

Further submissions in this direction should satisfy at least one of these:

- formalize a named theorem from the Markov-uniqueness literature;
- prove a classical restricted uniqueness case with an explicit arithmetic hypothesis;
- turn an existing accepted reduction into a strictly stronger reduction with a measurable
  loss of cases;
- package a genuinely reusable arithmetic object used by more than the conjecture itself.

A proposed theorem should state clearly how it sits relative to the open
`UniquenessConjecture`. No submission should imply that tree-position injectivity, full-state
injectivity, modular restrictions or collision factorization resolve injectivity of the single
maximum-coordinate label.

Do not add another branch-separation invariant solely because it holds experimentally. At this
stage, literature search and a proof-level reason for the invariant come before formalization.

### From topological transitivity to a chaos theorem

Once the accepted corpus contains both:

- topological transitivity (or a stronger accepted property) for the tent/logistic system; and
- density of periodic points,

then a Devaney-style theorem becomes a good packaging target. Before both components are in the
accepted corpus, it is premature.

Use an explicit formal definition of the chosen notion of chaos. Different texts package
sensitivity, transitivity and periodic density differently; do not silently switch definitions.

### Furstenberg completion beyond the topology theorem

An actual equivalence between a topological/uniform completion of the named Furstenberg
topological group and Mathlib's profinite completion is a valuable endpoint, but it may require
more uniform-space infrastructure than the topology comparison itself. Treat it as the second
stage of Direction A, not as an all-or-nothing requirement.

### Apollonian integral orbits

After Direction E supplies a clean matrix/group action, arithmetic questions about integral
Descartes quadruples, orbit preservation, primitive packings or congruence restrictions become
natural. Do not start them on the current four-scalar `IsQuadruple` representation merely to
avoid designing the group action.

## 5. Directions deliberately not promoted

The following are currently poor default targets:

- more Markov scaffolding without a named classical theorem or bounded restricted result;
- the bare corollary that a Markov number is a sum of two squares, unless it is part of a
  stronger reusable arithmetic interface;
- more Horadam/Fibonacci/Lucas identities that are short specializations of accepted recurrence
  and companion-matrix APIs;
- a graph-theoretic restatement of cyclic change ringing that adds only a new name for the
  already accepted adjacent-swap cycle;
- isolated separation-axiom corollaries for the Furstenberg topology now that its additive and
  finite-quotient structures are accepted;
- `FiniteGroupCharacter` extensions whose content is already available more generally in
  Mathlib's finite-abelian Fourier/character theory;
- direct `Padovan` + `Tribonacci` bridges without a compelling higher-order recurrence
  abstraction and a concrete theorem that consumes it;
- broad “chaos” declarations for the logistic map that do not specify and prove the component
  properties;
- direct equality between Descartes and inversive `reflect` operations without a geometric
  circle bridge;
- additional Calkin-Wilf/Stern-Brocot statements that only say the trees enumerate the same
  rationals; the accepted path-reversal theorem already captures that level of structure;
- folder-based bridges, such as connecting two modules merely because they live under the same
  top-level namespace.

Keep isolated modules isolated until a real theorem justifies a connection.

## 6. Greenfield work while mature clusters cool

This roadmap is not an exclusive work queue. In particular, a mature cluster may need time for
review, literature work or representation design while unrelated mathematics can still extend
the frontier.

For a greenfield theorem, prefer:

- a classical result with a stable statement and identifiable source;
- no equivalent theorem in the pinned Mathlib release;
- enough proof content that the submission is not a disguised exercise;
- a representation likely to support at least one natural follow-up theorem;
- a subject not chosen merely because it is easy to formalize in isolation.

A greenfield module does not need to import an existing LeanFrontier theorem. If it later gains a
consumer, that is stronger evidence of organic accumulation than forcing a connection at birth.

## 7. Per-target research dossier format

Before implementation, create a concise dossier containing:

- exact proposed Lean theorem statement or API shape;
- accepted LeanFrontier prerequisites and exact declarations expected to be reused;
- mathematical source(s) and what they actually establish;
- Mathlib search results for equivalent or supporting theorems;
- intended downstream consumers;
- edge cases / degenerate inputs / sign or representation traps;
- expected proof difficulty and missing infrastructure;
- explicit reason the result is more than a graph-padding import edge;
- go / reformulate / reject decision.

Only after that dossier survives review should implementation begin.

When refreshing this document, describe what is present in the **accepted corpus** and what
mathematical interfaces are missing. Do not encode contributor-specific branch states,
pull-request queues or submission sequencing; those are transient and belong in the relevant
issue or pull request, not in a shared mathematical roadmap.

A roadmap-refresh pull request should also state its own provenance: who or what selected the
directions, which accepted revision was audited, which Mathlib/literature searches informed the
reevaluation, and how much mathematical direction came from the human operator. That provenance
describes the planning process; it is not a receiver-validated mathematical claim.
